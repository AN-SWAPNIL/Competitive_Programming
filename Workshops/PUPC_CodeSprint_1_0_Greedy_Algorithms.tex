\sect{The greedy idea}
A greedy algorithm repeatedly makes a \textbf{locally best feasible choice} and commits to it. It gives an optimal answer \textbf{only when the greedy choice can be justified}.\par
\sect{Example 1: When greedy works}
\textbf{Activity selection.} Choose the maximum number of non-overlapping meetings. Meetings that touch at endpoints are compatible.\par\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Greedy rule:} Sort meetings by finishing time; take the next one that starts after (or exactly when) the last selected meeting ends.\par\vspace{8pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\rowcolors{2}{wash}{white}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}l l X l@{}}
\rowcolor{soft}\textcolor{ink}{Meeting}&\textcolor{ink}{Time}&\textcolor{ink}{Decision (in finish-time order)}&\textcolor{ink}{Selected}\\\midrule
A&[1,3)&Finish first&Yes\\
B&[2,5)&Overlaps A&No\\
C&[4,6)&Starts after A ends&Yes\\
D&[6,8)&Starts when C ends&Yes\\
E&[5,9)&Overlaps selected meetings&No\\
F&[8,10)&Starts when D ends&Yes\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}\par\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{Result:} A $\rightarrow$ C $\rightarrow$ D $\rightarrow$ F: \textbf{4 meetings}.\par\vspace{3pt}
{\color{muted}\small Why it works: An earliest-finishing available meeting leaves at least as much room for future meetings as any alternative first choice.}\par
\sect{Example 2: When a greedy rule fails}
\textbf{Coin change.} Coin denominations: $\{1,3,4\}$; target sum: $6$. Try choosing the largest coin that fits each time.\par\vspace{9pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{28mm} X >{\RaggedLeft\arraybackslash}p{19mm}@{}}
\rowcolor{wash}Largest-first&$6\rightarrow 4\; (2\text{ left})\rightarrow 1\; (1\text{ left})\rightarrow 1$&3 coins\\[5pt]
Optimal&$3+3=6$&2 coins\\
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{10pt}
\callout{Key point}{A locally appealing choice is not always optimal. \textbf{State your greedy rule, look for counterexamples, and justify why your choice is safe.} For arbitrary coin denominations, dynamic programming may be needed.}

\noindent{\color{ink}\bfseries A typical greedy workflow}\par\vspace{3pt}
{\small \textbf{Preprocess} (sort/count) $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Choose and update} (greedy steps) $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Postprocess} (restore indices/output). Not every problem uses all three stages.}\par\vspace{4pt}
{\color{muted}\small Example: in \emph{Restore the Weather}, sort, match in sorted order, then restore the original positions.}\par\vspace{4pt}
{\small \textbf{Why is the choice safe?} Exchange: swap without loss. \quad Forced choice: alternatives cannot be optimal. \quad Invariant: maintain a useful property.}\par
